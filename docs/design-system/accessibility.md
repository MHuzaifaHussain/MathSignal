# Accessibility

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes. `npm run check:design` enforces the color rules below on every build.

## Contrast

| Kind | Minimum | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Text | 4.5:1 | Body, muted text, labels, μ/σ symbols, line numbers |
| Graphics | 3:1 | Board frame against the page, axes, plot strokes, focus indicators |
| Code tokens | 5.5:1 | Every syntax color, so Expressive Code never alters it |

Yellow can't reach 3:1 on light surfaces. In light mode "look here" is ochre for lines and yellow only as a fill with ink on top.

## Color blindness

- Meaning is never carried by color alone. μ and σ also have a symbol or name, and in code a bold weight and a ring.
- Code colors keep a CIEDE2000 distance of at least 20 from μ, σ and attention for normal vision, and at least 12 under simulated deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia (Machado 2009).
- μ and σ are only 13 apart under protanopia, which is why σ always has its name or symbol next to it.

## Keyboard and screen readers

- Every board has a tray slider for each draggable handle, with `aria-valuetext` in words ("mean 1.50").
- Discrete boards (EventSpace) are a native table with `role="grid"`: one tab stop with a roving tabindex, arrow keys without wrapping, Home/End, Ctrl+Home/End, Space/Enter to toggle. Each outcome is a `<button aria-pressed>` whose name ends with its membership in words ("HT. First coin heads, second coin tails. In A."). Cells are at least 44px at a 375px viewport and never under 36px at 320px, with a 6px gap so the focus halo never touches a neighbor. Bursts of toggles announce once, after a 600ms pause, with the final count. Refused actions stay focusable (`aria-disabled`) and point at a visible reason. Fixed-data grids (a class of children) drop `aria-pressed` and mark their cells `aria-disabled`: they can be focused and read, not toggled; confirm the wording screen readers use for them in the NVDA/VoiceOver pass. While "Shrink the world to B" is on, ruled-out cells say "Ruled out." in their names, the status line says how many outcomes are left, and every change is announced as the new P(A | B), matching the readout.
- EventSpace's compare mode adds a "Compare A and B" switch (`role="switch"`, 44px) described by the visible note that says what it reveals. Turning it on announces the verdict, the exclusivity line and the product line; focus stays on the switch, because it stays on screen (unlike Check). "Shrink the world to B" is refused until Compare is on, with the visible reason "Turn on Compare A and B first: shrinking to B would answer the question for you." While Compare is on, a change to A or B announces what it did (the new count, or the new P(A | B) while shrunk, or why shrinking was undone), then "Independent: … Mutually exclusive: yes/no." A loaded die is chosen with a native radio group ("Die"), and each weighted cell's name ends with its units ("6. Face 6. In B. 7 of 12 units."). Fixed weather days read like the class of children. The comparison block prints only if Compare was on, so the page prose after each board states the answer.
- DistributionBoard ([decision 0012](../decisions/0012-distribution-board.md)) uses buttons in normal tab order, not `role="grid"`. Each outcome has a − and a + button named for what it does ("More candies for face 4", "Fewer candies for face 4"; "Raise X for HT"), and a row's value is read as a sentence ("Face 4: 1 candy."). The pairs sit in a group described by the status line. Refused presses stay focusable (`aria-disabled`) and point at a visible reason beside the steppers ("Press Balance it first, then change the values.", "A face can't win fewer than 0 candies."), never only in a tooltip or the live region. Bursts of presses announce once after a short pause, with the bar the outcome landed in and its chance. Each button is at least 44px, and never under 36px at 320px.
- The pencil has a keyboard path: the tray's "Pencil position" slider, with `aria-valuetext` in words ("pencil at 1.17 candies"). The strip under the axis is only a pointer shortcut to the same value, so it is `aria-hidden` and never focusable. A late slider commit never talks over a button pressed since. "Balance it" is refused while the pencil is parked, with a visible reason, and moves focus to the verdict sentence once it answers.
- The chart is `aria-hidden`. Its equivalents are the readout sentences (one per bar, plus the total), which carry everything the table does, and a native table with column and row headers (`Value`, the outcomes, `P(X = x)`) behind Table view that also prints. On goal setups the chart, readouts and table are not rendered until Check passes, so no screen-reader user can reach the answer early. A covered panel says "The chart appears when your values match the card." Because Check and "Check goal" disappear once they pass, focus moves to the sentence that answers them. Tilt, μ and σ are always in words and numbers as well as the drawing.
- DistributionBoard's family and density modes ([decision 0018](../decisions/0018-distribution-families-and-density.md)) keep every control native and in tab order: tray sliders, radio groups for the guess, "Show as" and "Bar width", and buttons for the presets, the read pair, Check, the switches and Reset. No slider's range ever changes. The "Read the bar for" pair refuses at its ends with a visible reason ("10 coins can't show more than 10 heads."), and From and To never refuse: moving one past the other pushes it along, and the announcement says so ("To moved along to 3 cm."). A value never changes silently: when n drops, the read bar clamps and the sentence says it is "the most 2 coins can show". On `coins-binomial`, from 4 coins on, the chart, readouts, table and Table view switch are not rendered until Check, and presets are refused with "Lock in your guess first."; Check and the guess radios leave after Check, which moves focus to the verdict. Picking a guess announces nothing beyond the native radio. Sequences ("HHT"), superscript powers and "≤" have spoken forms ("first coin heads, second coin heads, third coin tails", "0.9 to the power 9", "the chance that X is from 1 to 2 cm"). Any nonzero chance draws at least 2px and reads "under 0.01", never "0.00". In forced colors the columns and curve draw in CanvasText, the read column keeps a 3px Highlight ring and the shaded stretch fills with a hatch pattern.
- VectorBoard2D ([decision 0014](../decisions/0014-vector-board.md)) has two keyboard paths for every handle. The Mafs handles are focusable and named ("Tip of trip a. It also moves with the buttons under the plane. Use the arrow keys to move it."), and one arrow press moves one grid unit. The step strip under the plane is the dependable path, always visible: one group per movable vector, named by it ("Trip a", "a"), with − / + buttons in normal tab order ("Move a 1 east"), grouped per axis ("Move a east or west"); its column words are `aria-hidden`, since every button names its direction. The read-only coordinate table, a native table with a caption and a Length column, is behind Table view and always prints; it is the screen-reader path to the lengths on `trips-add`. Refused presses stay focusable and point at a visible note ("a can't go further east: the board ends at 5.", "a and b are the question. Press Check first."). The live region speaks when a drag or arrow move ends, after a short pause for a burst of − / + presses, once per slider change, and at once for buttons; it names an edge the handle reached and handles that sit on one point. Announcements say the symbols in words, as the notation registry does ("a dot b", "the length of a", "u hat", "theta"), because screen readers at their default punctuation level skip ‖ and ·; the visible readouts keep the symbols. Locked vectors have no handle at all before Check, so nothing can be dragged out of turn. The a + b row, shadows, angles and presets aren't rendered until Check, and focus moves to the verdict when Check disappears. The sign guess is a native radio group, and Check is refused with a visible reason until a sign is picked. On `span-reach` the two tray sliders, "Scale c" and "Scale d" ("scale d, 1"), are the keyboard path to the answer, and a late commit from one never talks over the other; on `span-shape` the span guess is a native radio group, refused the same way. The readouts say whatever the slider sends past the board's edge, so the cut arrows lose nothing for a screen-reader user. On boards under 400px the steppers keep 44px height and narrow from 44px toward 36px wide; the folded table, Length column included, fits the tray at 320px without scrolling sideways. In forced colors the board maps its ink, muted ink and ochre to system colors, because SVG strokes aren't forced.
- BayesTheorem's tray sliders and preset buttons are its keyboard path; the Mafs areas have no handles. Announcements use the shared announcer and say the symbols in words ("Posterior P of A given B is 0.083; P of B is 0.108."). Its "Table view" switch ([decision 0016](../decisions/0016-bayes-board-presets-and-table.md)) is the shared fold (decision 0017): turning it on announces "Table shown under the switch." and what the table counts ("Out of 1,000 people, 108 are in B and 9 of those are in A."), and while it is on every change adds that sentence. The table is native, with a caption and row and column headers; its two summary lines show "P(A | B)" to sighted readers and give screen readers "P of A given B". Off, the table is `display: none` on screen and in the accessibility tree, and it always prints. Bold, not color, marks the two counts P(A | B) divides, so forced colors lose nothing.
- DescriptiveStats ([decision 0015](../decisions/0015-descriptive-stats-board.md)) uses native controls in normal tab order: radio groups ("Your pick for the middle", "Child to move", "Scale", "Flag when |z| is above", and the stage choice), tray sliders with values in words ("child 4, 131 cm", "newcomer has 1,000 thousand dollars, a million", "giant pumpkin, 30 kg"), switches (`role="switch"`: "Line them up", "Show distances", "Also as a sample", "The millionaire walks in") and preset buttons. Radio options name their child ("Child 7"), so each makes sense on its own; naming each height too made the tray 96px taller at 375px, so the table behind Table view is the screen-reader path to each value. The drag strip under the `class-spread` axis is only a pointer shortcut to the Height slider, so it is `aria-hidden` and never focusable. Check is refused, with a visible reason, until a child is picked, and focus moves to the verdict once Check or "Show the answer" disappears. Buttons, radios and switches announce at once; slider and drag changes announce once, after they end, and a late slider commit never talks over a button pressed since. The chart is `aria-hidden`; its equivalent is the readouts, plus the tables behind Table view (a native data table and a summary table: Statistic, Value, How it is computed), which all print. The readouts never list every value, so the tables are part of the equivalent. On `class-lineup` the summary rows and the "Place in line" column are not rendered until Check, and the reason is a readout line under the chart. Summary rows and readouts that would answer a page's prediction wait for the step, scale or giant that commits to it (decision 0015). Symbols lines show Unicode (x₍₁₎, Σ) to sighted readers and give screen readers a spoken form instead; confirm both in the NVDA/VoiceOver pass. On boards under 400px only every other axis number is kept, mark labels sit on rows of their own so they never collide, and tray sliders put their label on its own line. In forced colors dots, bars and the median diamond draw in CanvasText and the "look here" dot keeps a Highlight ring; the mean and median stay apart by shape and word, the flags by solid and dashed rings and their tags.
- NormalDistribution's z mode ([decision 0019](../decisions/0019-normal-distribution-z-mode.md)) keeps the handle out of the tab order; the tray's Height slider is its path (1 cm per arrow, 5 cm per Page Up or Down; "Value z" on the standard preset says negatives in words: "z minus 1.25"). Before Check the tab stops are the slider, the "Who is taller for their age" radios, Check, Table view and Reset; after it, the presets, the slider, "Shade the share below", Table view and Reset. Check is refused with a visible reason until a radio is picked, and focus moves to the verdict when it disappears. Lines whose spoken form differs ("z is minus 12 divided by 8, which is minus 1.5", "Phi of 7 over 12 is about 0.7202") show the symbols and give screen readers the words, the verdict included, and a passed Check announces nothing because the focused verdict is read there. The chart is `aria-hidden`; its equivalents are the readouts and the table behind Table view. Nothing that gives a z-score is rendered before Check, folded or not. In forced colors μ and σ both draw in CanvasText and stay apart by dashed against solid and by their labels.
- FunctionCurve ([decision 0020](../decisions/0020-function-curve-board.md)) keeps the point's handle out of the tab order; the tray sliders are the complete path: "Your slope at x = 1" ("your slope 2.25"), "Ruler length h" ("h equals minus 0.25, ruler to x equals 0.75"), "Point x" or "Point w" ("x equals 1.5, y equals 2.25", "w equals 0, loss 9") and "Line slope s" ("line slope 0.5"). Stages and the corner question are native radio groups, "Lines under the corner" a `role="switch"`. Controls that are the question or would reveal it (Point x, the presets, the shorter rulers, Tangent, Slope curve, the corner switch) aren't rendered before Check. Every line and preset button holding −, ², ³, = or |x| shows the symbols and gives screen readers a spoken twin ("y equals x squared", "minus 0.75", "V: the absolute value of x at 0"); the guess slider's and the corner question's names are still plain text until the screen reader pass (decision 0020). The live region speaks when a slider commit or a drag ends and at once for buttons, radios and switches; a late slider commit never talks over a press since. Check and "Show the answer" move focus to the verdict when they disappear. The plane's equivalents are the status line, the readout lines (point, ruler, edge sentence, tangent, corner line) and the table behind Table view.
- Every board with a data table folds it behind one "Table view" switch ([decision 0017](../decisions/0017-table-view-fold.md)): a `role="switch"` button, the last tray item before Reset, with the table region directly after it in the DOM. Turning it on says "Table shown under the switch." and what the table holds; turning it off says "Table hidden.". Folded, the table is `display: none` and out of the accessibility tree; it always prints. A fold holds no control, so folding never strands focus, and it is never a spoiler gate: answers stay unrendered until the reader commits, folded or not. Reset moves below an open table; it is never reordered with CSS.
- Board figures have an accessible name (`Board`'s `label`).
- A table that can be wider than its column sits in a focusable region named by its caption (`role="region"`, `tabindex="0"`, `aria-labelledby` the caption), so a keyboard reader can scroll it with the arrow keys; `PropertiesTable` does this, with row headers (`th scope="row"`). The region never clips on the block axis, so the focus halo shows, and in print it never clips at all. Markdown tables, NotationKey and display formulas that are wider than the column become focusable and named while they overflow, and only then ([decision 0024](../decisions/0024-focusable-scroll-boxes.md)); a table or formula wider than the 632px desktop column is rewritten to fit.
- Live regions are polite and update when an interaction ends, never on every frame.
- The focus ring is visible on every surface (ink outline plus yellow halo).
- Interactive targets are at least 44px.
- KaTeX keeps its hidden MathML output for screen readers.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Manual checks before merging a visual change

1. Both themes, at 375px and 1280px wide.
2. Keyboard only: Tab through the header, sidebar, every board control and Run button.
3. Zoom to 200%: nothing overlaps or scrolls sideways.
4. A board on a dim or low-contrast screen: the frame still separates it from the page.
