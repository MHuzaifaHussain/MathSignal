# Accessibility

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes. `npm run check:design` enforces the color rules below on every build.

## Contrast

| Kind | Minimum | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Text | 4.5:1 | Body, muted text, labels, μ/σ symbols, line numbers |
| Graphics | 3:1 | Board frame against the page, axes, plot strokes, focus indicators |
| Code tokens | 5.5:1 | Every syntax color, so Expressive Code never alters it |

Yellow can't reach 3:1 on light surfaces. In light mode "look here" is ochre for lines and yellow only as a fill with ink on top.

## Color blindness

- Meaning is never carried by color alone. μ and σ also have a symbol or name, and in code a bold weight and a ring.
- Code colors keep a CIEDE2000 distance of at least 20 from μ, σ and attention for normal vision, and at least 12 under simulated deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia (Machado 2009).
- μ and σ are only 13 apart under protanopia, which is why σ always has its name or symbol next to it.

## Keyboard and screen readers

- Every board has a tray slider for each draggable handle, with `aria-valuetext` in words ("mean 1.50").
- Discrete boards (EventSpace) are a native table with `role="grid"`: one tab stop with a roving tabindex, arrow keys without wrapping, Home/End, Ctrl+Home/End, Space/Enter to toggle. Each outcome is a `<button aria-pressed>` whose name ends with its membership in words ("HT. First coin heads, second coin tails. In A."). Cells are at least 44px at a 375px viewport and never under 36px at 320px, with a 6px gap so the focus halo never touches a neighbor. Bursts of toggles announce once, after a 600ms pause, with the final count. Refused actions stay focusable (`aria-disabled`) and point at a visible reason. Fixed-data grids (a class of children) drop `aria-pressed` and mark their cells `aria-disabled`: they can be focused and read, not toggled; confirm the wording screen readers use for them in the NVDA/VoiceOver pass. While "Shrink the world to B" is on, ruled-out cells say "Ruled out." in their names, the status line says how many outcomes are left, and every change is announced as the new P(A | B), matching the readout.
- EventSpace's compare mode adds a "Compare A and B" switch (`role="switch"`, 44px) described by the visible note that says what it reveals. Turning it on announces the verdict, the exclusivity line and the product line; focus stays on the switch, because it stays on screen (unlike Check). "Shrink the world to B" is refused until Compare is on, with the visible reason "Turn on Compare A and B first: shrinking to B would answer the question for you." While Compare is on, a change to A or B announces what it did (the new count, or the new P(A | B) while shrunk, or why shrinking was undone), then "Independent: … Mutually exclusive: yes/no." A loaded die is chosen with a native radio group ("Die"), and each weighted cell's name ends with its units ("6. Face 6. In B. 7 of 12 units."). Fixed weather days read like the class of children. The comparison block prints only if Compare was on, so the page prose after each board states the answer.
- DistributionBoard ([decision 0012](../decisions/0012-distribution-board.md)) uses buttons in normal tab order, not `role="grid"`. Each outcome has a − and a + button named for what it does ("More candies for face 4", "Fewer candies for face 4"; "Raise X for HT"), and a row's value is read as a sentence ("Face 4: 1 candy."). The pairs sit in a group described by the status line. Refused presses stay focusable (`aria-disabled`) and point at a visible reason beside the steppers ("Press Balance it first, then change the values.", "A face can't win fewer than 0 candies."), never only in a tooltip or the live region. Bursts of presses announce once after a short pause, with the bar the outcome landed in and its chance. Each button is at least 44px, and never under 36px at 320px.
- The pencil has a keyboard path: the tray's "Pencil position" slider, with `aria-valuetext` in words ("pencil at 1.17 candies"). The strip under the axis is only a pointer shortcut to the same value, so it is `aria-hidden` and never focusable. A late slider commit never talks over a button pressed since. "Balance it" is refused while the pencil is parked, with a visible reason, and moves focus to the verdict sentence once it answers.
- The chart is `aria-hidden`. Its equivalents are the readout sentences (one per bar, plus the total) and a native table with column and row headers (`Value`, the outcomes, `P(X = x)`) that also prints. On goal setups the chart, readouts and table are not rendered until Check passes, so no screen-reader user can reach the answer early. A covered panel says "The chart appears when your values match the card." Because Check and "Check goal" disappear once they pass, focus moves to the sentence that answers them. Tilt, μ and σ are always in words and numbers as well as the drawing.
- VectorBoard2D ([decision 0014](../decisions/0014-vector-board.md)) has two keyboard paths for every handle. The Mafs handles are focusable and named ("Tip of vector a. Its position is in the table below. Use the arrow keys to move it."), and one arrow press moves one grid unit. The coordinate table under the plane is the dependable path for screen readers in browse mode: a native table with a caption, whose movable rows have − / + buttons in normal tab order ("Move a 1 east"), grouped per cell ("Move a east or west"). Refused presses stay focusable and point at a visible note ("a can't go further east: the board ends at 5.", "a and b are the question. Press Check first."). The live region speaks when a drag or arrow move ends, after a short pause for a burst of − / + presses, once per slider change, and at once for buttons; it names an edge the handle reached and handles that sit on one point. Announcements say the symbols in words, as the notation registry does ("a dot b", "the length of a", "u hat", "theta"), because screen readers at their default punctuation level skip ‖ and ·; the visible readouts keep the symbols. Locked vectors have no handle at all before Check, so nothing can be dragged out of turn. The a + b row, shadows, angles and presets aren't rendered until Check, and focus moves to the verdict when Check disappears. The sign guess is a native radio group, and Check is refused with a visible reason until a sign is picked. On boards under 400px the steppers keep 44px height and narrow from 44px toward 36px wide, and the Length column moves into sentences under the table so it never scrolls sideways. In forced colors the board maps its ink, muted ink and ochre to system colors, because SVG strokes aren't forced.
- DescriptiveStats ([decision 0015](../decisions/0015-descriptive-stats-board.md)) uses native controls in normal tab order: radio groups ("Your middle child", "Child to move", "Scale", "Flag when |z| is above", and the stage choice), tray sliders with values in words ("child 4, 131 cm", "newcomer has 1,000 thousand dollars, a million", "giant pumpkin, 30 kg"), switches (`role="switch"`: "Line them up", "Show distances", "Also as a sample", "The millionaire walks in") and preset buttons. Radio options name their child ("Child 7"), so each makes sense on its own. The drag strip under the `class-spread` axis is only a pointer shortcut to the Height slider, so it is `aria-hidden` and never focusable. Check is refused, with a visible reason, until a child is picked, and focus moves to the verdict once Check or "Show the answer" disappears. Buttons, radios and switches announce at once; slider and drag changes announce once, after they end, and a late slider commit never talks over a button pressed since. The chart is `aria-hidden`; its equivalents are the readout sentences, a native data table and a summary table (Statistic, Value, How it is computed), which all print. On `class-lineup` the summary rows and the "Place in line" column are not rendered until Check. Symbols lines show Unicode (x₍₁₎, Σ) to sighted readers and give screen readers a spoken form instead; confirm both in the NVDA/VoiceOver pass. On boards under 400px only every other axis number is kept, mark labels sit on rows of their own so they never collide, and tray sliders put their label on its own line. In forced colors dots, bars and the median diamond draw in CanvasText and the "look here" dot keeps a Highlight ring; the mean and median stay apart by shape and word, the flags by solid and dashed rings and their tags.
- Board figures have an accessible name (`Board`'s `label`).
- Live regions are polite and update when an interaction ends, never on every frame.
- The focus ring is visible on every surface (ink outline plus yellow halo).
- Interactive targets are at least 44px.
- KaTeX keeps its hidden MathML output for screen readers.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Manual checks before merging a visual change

1. Both themes, at 375px and 1280px wide.
2. Keyboard only: Tab through the header, sidebar, every board control and Run button.
3. Zoom to 200%: nothing overlaps or scrolls sideways.
4. A board on a dim or low-contrast screen: the frame still separates it from the page.
